{% load i18n %}{% comment %} THE JURISDICTION BAR — who is answerable for each stretch of the process. The one device the Design Council asked for unanimously: all four judges, with four different mandates, named it independently as the element to carry into the selected direction. Their argument was that a register device and a route rule both encode SEQUENCE, which every process diagram on the web encodes, while this encodes JURISDICTION — which is this project's actual subject, and the thing a person who has already been approached by someone claiming to act for an administration most needs to be able to read. It is also the graphic answer to the product's own acceptance criterion: "chi è responsabile". Four owners, four textures. The texture is not decoration: a reader who cannot use colour, a reader in greyscale and a reader under forced colours all still get the answer, because the hatch and the legend carry it. The legend is a list, so a screen reader reads owners and stretches in order. Contract: `segments` = list of {owner, label, share} where owner is one of morocco | italy | platform | authority, and share is an integer from 1 to 6 (relative width). The share travels as a data attribute, not as an inline style: the public Content-Security-Policy is self-only with no unsafe-inline, so a style attribute would be silently dropped in production and the bar would render with four equal segments — a diagram quietly telling the wrong story.
