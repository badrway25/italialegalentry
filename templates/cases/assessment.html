This is the page that answers "why was I told this", and it is the difference between an orientation and an oracle. It reads the SNAPSHOT, never the current rules: an outcome given in October must still explain itself in March, when the rules have moved on without it. Three things are shown, in this order, because that is the order in which a reader builds trust: 1. the outcome itself, in words, never as a decision — its rules version and date, then the clause 2. the RULES that produced it, each named and versioned (the registers' sequence: their order of application, each place in ILE Mono), and the requirements found missing 3. the SOURCES those rules stand on, numbered, with their precise references and then whether the result replays: a section on the page's own ground — never V1's night verso, whose text stood at 1.06 : 1 on the case's pre-assessment (W3-F1's finding) —, a divergence said as a warning. The head: «Pré-évaluation», then the file it belongs to — «Dossier», its reference in ILE Mono, its state — on the head's line of facts: the reference is context, not an eyebrow over the title (C-4). The outcome carries the glyph of its place on the journey — «La soglia»'s «Pré-évaluation et parcours possibles», the dashboard's and the case file's glyph for this section (plan §3.1 item 7: the screen's carrier at rest; the review's minor 10). The case band of the V4 shell is not drawn: the head says the file once. V1's folio labels and ordinals left with the redraw. An empty source list is stated rather than hidden. An outcome that cannot cite anything is still an honest outcome; one that quietly shows no sources invites the reader to assume they exist.
